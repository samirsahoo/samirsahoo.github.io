FinTech Regulations for Architects

What financial regulations mean for system design: PCI DSS v4.0.1, DORA and NIS2, PSD2 and PSD3/PSR with SCA and open banking, the Instant Payments Regulation, GDPR, the EU AI Act after the 2026 Digital Omnibus, the Cyber Resilience Act, UK and US rules, India's DPDP Act and Rules 2025, RBI and CERT-In directions, SEBI CSCRF, SWIFT CSP and BCBS 239, plus how to map controls once to many regulations.

Simple definition#

FinTech regulations set legally binding requirements for how financial services protect money, data and customers, and how they stay operational. For architects, every regulation turns into non-functional requirements: security controls, data handling, resilience, auditability and reporting timelines.

WarningNot legal advice

This page summarises regulations as they affect system design, as of November 2026. Dates and details change. Confirm applicability and obligations with compliance and legal teams and the primary texts.

The landscape at a glance#

PCI DSS v4.0.1 (cards)#

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard applies to anyone who stores, processes or transmits cardholder data.

  • v4.0.1 (June 2024) is the only active version. Requirements that were “future-dated” became mandatory on 31 March 2025.
  • The 12 requirements fall into six goals: secure network, protect account data, vulnerability management, strong access control, monitoring and testing, security policy.
  • Architecturally important v4 requirements:
    • 8.4.2: MFA for all access into the cardholder data environment (CDE).
    • 6.4.3 and 11.6.1: inventory, authorise and integrity-check payment page scripts, and detect unauthorised changes. This counters Magecart-style skimming.
    • 5.4.1: anti-phishing mechanisms.
    • 3.5.1.1: keyed cryptographic hashes for PAN.
    • 12.5.2: annual scope confirmation (every six months for service providers).
    • The customised approach: meet a control objective in your own way, with a targeted risk analysis.
  • Design lever: scope reduction. Tokenise PAN, use hosted payment fields or iframes and P2PE, and segment the network. Systems that see only tokens fall out of scope.
  • PCI SSC ran a request for comments on the standard in mid-2026 to inform the next version, so watch for updates.

DORA (EU Digital Operational Resilience Act)#

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 has applied since 17 January 2025 to banks, insurers, investment firms, payment and e-money institutions, crypto-asset service providers and their critical ICT providers. It is the lex specialis over NIS2 for the financial sector.

Pillar What it requires architecturally
ICT risk management An ICT risk framework; asset inventory; protection, detection, response and recovery; backup and restoration testing; segregation; change management
Incident management and reporting Classify incidents; major ICT incidents: initial notification within 4 hours of classification (and within 24 hours of becoming aware), intermediate report within 72 hours, final report within one month
Resilience testing Annual testing programme; threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) every 3 years for significant entities (TIBER-EU based; RTS adopted)
ICT third-party risk Register of information for all ICT contracts; mandatory contract clauses; exit strategies; concentration risk
Information sharing Voluntary threat intelligence sharing
  • The ESAs designated the first critical ICT third-party providers (CTPPs), including major cloud and technology providers, in November 2025. They are now under direct EU oversight.
  • Design implications: documented dependency maps, tested failover and restore, immutable logging for incident timelines, automated incident classification, and exit plans for cloud services (multi-cloud or portability where justified).

NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) applies to many other essential and important entities with similar requirements: risk measures, 24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification. Financial entities follow DORA instead.

Payments: PSD2 → PSD3 / PSR, and instant payments#

PSD2 (in force since 2018) introduced:

  • Strong customer authentication (SCA): two of knowledge, possession, inherence, with dynamic linking (an authentication code tied to amount and payee) for remote payments. Exemptions exist for low value, trusted beneficiaries and transaction risk analysis.
  • Open banking (access to accounts): banks must give licensed third parties (AISPs, PISPs) secure API access. This drove standards such as Berlin Group NextGenPSD2, UK Open Banking and FAPI security profiles.

PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation (PSR): political agreement was reached in November 2025, and final compromise texts were published in April 2026. Formal adoption is expected in late 2026, with application likely in 2027–2028. Expected changes:

  • Fraud: liability shifts for certain impersonation scams (for example fraudsters posing as bank staff), fraud data sharing between providers, transaction monitoring requirements.
  • SCA improvements: accessibility (not only smartphones), clearer rules for merchant-initiated transactions.
  • Open banking: dedicated-interface performance rules and permission dashboards for customers.
  • Payment and e-money institutions merge into one licensing regime.

EU Instant Payments Regulation (2024/886): euro-area PSPs must send instant payments (from 9 October 2025), at the same price as standard transfers. Verification of Payee (IBAN–name check) has been required since October 2025. Sanctions screening is done daily against customer bases rather than per transaction.

GDPR (EU personal data)#

  • Principles: lawfulness, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, security, accountability.
  • Data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection, rights on automated decisions under Article 22).
  • Privacy by design and default (Article 25); DPIAs for high-risk processing; records of processing.
  • Breach notification to the supervisory authority within 72 hours.
  • International transfers: adequacy decisions (including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework), SCCs, BCRs.
  • Fines of up to €20M or 4% of global turnover.
  • The EU’s 2025–26 Digital Omnibus proposals also include targeted GDPR amendments. Track their final form.

See data security and privacy for engineering patterns.

EU AI Act#

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in force since August 2024, takes a risk-based approach:

Tier Examples in finance Obligations
Prohibited (since Feb 2025) Social scoring, manipulative techniques, some biometric uses Banned
High-risk Credit scoring and creditworthiness of individuals; life and health insurance risk pricing; some HR uses Risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, transparency, human oversight, accuracy and robustness and cybersecurity, conformity assessment, registration
Transparency (Art. 50, from 2 Aug 2026) Chatbots, AI-generated content Tell users they are interacting with AI; label synthetic content
General-purpose AI models (since Aug 2025) Foundation model providers Documentation, copyright policy; systemic-risk models face more
Minimal risk Most other uses Voluntary codes
  • Timeline change (2026 Digital Omnibus on AI): the EU agreed to postpone high-risk obligations. Standalone Annex III systems (such as credit scoring) now apply from 2 December 2027, and AI in regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028. Transparency and GPAI obligations were not delayed.
  • Fraud detection systems are explicitly not classed as high-risk under the credit-scoring category.
  • Fines of up to €35M or 7% for prohibited practices.
  • Design implications: model inventory, data lineage and quality evidence, bias testing, explainability for adverse decisions, logging of inputs and outputs, and human review workflows. See AI security.

EU Cyber Resilience Act#

This act applies to products with digital elements placed on the EU market, including software. It requires secure-by-design development, vulnerability handling, SBOMs and security updates. Vulnerability and incident reporting obligations apply from September 2026, and full application follows in December 2027. Vendors of banking software and devices must comply. Banks should ask for it in procurement.

United Kingdom and United States (brief)#

Jurisdiction Key rules for architects
UK FCA/PRA operational resilience (important business services, impact tolerances, mapping and testing; fully applicable since March 2025); Consumer Duty; mandatory APP fraud reimbursement (since October 2024); critical third parties regime; UK GDPR
US GLBA Safeguards Rule (information security programme, encryption, MFA); NYDFS 23 NYCRR 500 (amended 2023: MFA, asset inventory, CISO reporting, 72-hour notification); SEC cyber incident disclosure (material incidents within 4 business days); SOX IT general controls; FFIEC guidance; state privacy laws

India#

Rule Architectural requirements
DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025 (notified November 2025) Notice and consent (or legitimate uses); purpose limitation; reasonable security safeguards (encryption, access control, logging, one-year log retention); breach notification to the Data Protection Board and affected individuals; children’s data protections; rights to access, correction and erasure; Significant Data Fiduciaries: DPIAs, audits, possible localisation of specified data. Phased: the Board was set up immediately, Consent Manager provisions follow from November 2026, and most obligations apply from May 2027. Penalties up to ₹250 crore per breach category.
RBI cyber security directions (31 July 2026) Entity-specific Master Directions (commercial banks, SFBs, UCBs, payments banks, AIFIs) replacing the 2016 framework and the 2023 IT governance direction. They cover board-level governance, a cyber security policy, SOC, access control, patching, secure configuration, and incident reporting to RBI within 6 hours. Issued alongside Digital Payment Security Controls directions.
RBI data localisation (2018) All payment system data stored only in India
RBI IT outsourcing directions (2023) Due diligence, audit rights, concentration risk and exit plans for cloud and IT providers
RBI authentication directions for digital payments (2025) Two-factor authentication with more flexible factors (beyond SMS OTP) and risk-based checks, applicable from April 2026
RBI FREE-AI framework (August 2025) Principles and recommendations for responsible, ethical AI in the financial sector
CERT-In directions (2022) Report specified incidents within 6 hours; keep ICT logs for 180 days in India; synchronise clocks to NTP sources
SEBI CSCRF (2024) The Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework for SEBI-regulated entities (brokers, depositories, AMCs and others): graded by entity size; governance, identify, protect, detect, respond, recover; SOC requirements; phased compliance
NPCI / UPI rules Device binding, transaction limits, fraud monitoring and API standards for UPI participants

Cross-sector frameworks often required#

  • SWIFT Customer Security Programme (CSP/CSCF): mandatory and advisory controls for SWIFT users, attested annually.
  • BCBS 239: risk data aggregation and reporting. See data governance.
  • Basel operational resilience principles (2021) and ISO 27001 / SOC 2 for vendors.
  • ISO 20022 messaging: SWIFT cross-border migration completed in November 2025. Rich structured data helps screening and fraud detection.

Design once, comply many times#

Map one control to many obligations and collect evidence automatically:

Control PCI DSS DORA GDPR / DPDP RBI / CERT-In AI Act
MFA everywhere (phishing-resistant) 8.4 ICT risk (access) Security of processing Access control —
Tokenisation / encryption with HSM keys 3.x ICT risk (protection) Art. 32 / reasonable safeguards Data security —
Immutable, centralised logging (≥ 1 year) 10.x Incident detection, timelines Accountability, breach evidence 180-day logs in India (CERT-In) Logging for high-risk AI
Automated incident classification + 4/6/24/72 h runbooks 12.10 Incident reporting 72 h breach notice 6 h reporting Serious incident reporting
Third-party register + exit plans 12.8 Register of information Processor contracts Outsourcing directions Provider and deployer roles
Lineage + data quality — — Accuracy, rights requests — Data governance (Art. 10)
Threat modelling + secure SDLC 6.2 ICT change management Privacy by design Secure development Cybersecurity (Art. 15)

Compliance as code: policies in OPA or Cedar, cloud configuration checked against control baselines, evidence collected automatically from pipelines and logs, and continuous control monitoring dashboards.

Common mistakes#

Common mistakeTreating compliance as a yearly audit project

Controls drift between audits. Automate control checks and evidence collection.

Common mistakeIgnoring reporting clocks in incident design

A 4-hour (DORA) or 6-hour (RBI, CERT-In) clock is impossible without automated detection, classification and pre-approved templates.

Common mistakeCard data everywhere

Every system touching PAN is in PCI scope. Tokenise early to shrink scope.

Common mistakeForgetting AI in the compliance map

Credit scoring is high-risk under the EU AI Act. Plan documentation, oversight and bias testing before the 2027 deadline.

Quick check#

What became mandatory under PCI DSS v4.0.1 on 31 March 2025? The previously future-dated requirements, including MFA for all CDE access, payment page script controls (6.4.3 and 11.6.1), anti-phishing, and targeted risk analyses.

What are DORA’s five pillars? ICT risk management, incident management and reporting, resilience testing (including TLPT), ICT third-party risk, and information sharing.

What is the DORA initial notification deadline for a major ICT incident? Within 4 hours of classifying it as major, and no later than 24 hours after becoming aware of it.

When do EU AI Act high-risk obligations apply to credit scoring after the 2026 Digital Omnibus? From 2 December 2027, for standalone Annex III systems.

What does RBI require for payment data storage? Payment system data must be stored only in India.

What are the CERT-In incident reporting and log retention requirements? Report specified incidents within 6 hours, and retain ICT logs for 180 days within India.

What does PSD2 SCA require? Two independent factors from knowledge, possession and inherence, plus dynamic linking to amount and payee for remote payments.

Summary#

  • Regulations become NFRs: protect card and personal data, stay resilient, report fast, govern AI.
  • PCI DSS v4.0.1 and DORA are in force; PSD3/PSR and the AI Act’s high-risk rules arrive in 2027–28.
  • India adds DPDP (phased to 2027), RBI’s 2026 cyber directions, data localisation, CERT-In and SEBI CSCRF.
  • Map controls once to many regulations, and automate evidence.
