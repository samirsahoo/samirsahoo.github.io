Data Governance: Catalog, Lineage, Quality and Contracts

Making data findable, understood and trustworthy: governance operating model and roles, metadata and catalogs, lineage with OpenLineage, data quality dimensions and checks, data contracts, master and reference data, observability, regulatory reporting (BCBS 239) and AI data governance.

Simple definition#

Data governance is the set of roles, policies, standards and tools that make sure data is known (catalogued and defined), trusted (good quality with traceable lineage), protected (secure and private) and used properly (meeting regulation and ethics) across its lifecycle.

The problem#

“Why does the risk report say 4.2 billion and finance say 4.5?” Two teams use different definitions of “exposure”, sourced from different copies, with no record of the transformations in between. Regulators require banks to prove where reported numbers come from. AI models trained on unknown data create legal and reputational risk.

The operating model#

Role Accountability
Data owner Business accountability for a data domain: who may access it, quality targets
Data steward Definitions, glossary, quality rules, issue resolution
Data custodian Technical operation: storage, backup, security controls
Data product owner A data product’s contract, SLOs and roadmap (data mesh)

Reference frameworks include DAMA-DMBOK (the knowledge areas of data management) and the EDM Council DCAM / CDMC (capability models, including cloud data management controls).

Metadata and the catalog#

Metadata type Examples
Technical Schemas, tables, columns, types, partitions
Business Glossary terms (“active customer”), definitions, owners
Operational Freshness, volumes, job runs, quality scores
Security and privacy Classification (public, internal, confidential, restricted), PII tags, retention, legal basis

Catalogs: platform catalogs (Unity Catalog, Polaris, AWS Glue / DataZone, Google Dataplex, Microsoft Purview) and enterprise catalogs (Collibra, Alation, Atlan, plus open-source DataHub and OpenMetadata). Automate harvesting; manual catalogs go stale.

Lineage#

Lineage shows where data came from and what happened to it, from source system to report or model.

  • Column-level lineage is needed for regulatory reporting and impact analysis (“if I change this column, which reports break?”).
  • OpenLineage (an LF AI & Data project) is the open standard. Airflow, Spark, Flink, dbt and others emit run events; Marquez, DataHub and commercial catalogs collect them.
  • Use lineage for impact analysis, root-cause analysis of bad numbers, privacy (where does PII flow?) and evidence for auditors.

Data quality#

Dimension Question Example check
Completeness Is everything there? customer_id not null; row count within ±5% of yesterday
Validity Does it follow the rules? IBAN format, currency in ISO 4217
Accuracy Does it match reality? Reconcile balances with the core banking ledger
Consistency Does it agree across systems? Same customer status in CRM and core banking
Uniqueness No duplicates? One row per payment_id
Timeliness / freshness Is it recent enough? Gold table updated by 06:00

Tools: dbt tests, Great Expectations / GX, Soda, Deequ, and data observability platforms (Monte Carlo, Bigeye, and the observability features of the catalogs).

Where to check: at ingestion (schema, contract), between layers (silver rules) and before publishing a gold product. Failing a critical check should stop publication, not just alert.

Data contracts#

Contracts are agreements between producers and consumers covering schema, semantics, quality, SLOs and ownership. They move quality upstream to the source team. See Schemas and event contracts for the ODCS format and CI enforcement.

Master and reference data#

  • Master data (customer, product, counterparty, legal entity) needs a golden record, built from matching and survivorship rules in MDM tools or a well-governed gold product.
  • Reference data (currencies, country codes, LEIs, product hierarchies) must be versioned and shared centrally, not copied and edited per team.

Regulatory reporting: BCBS 239#

The Basel Committee’s BCBS 239 principles for risk data aggregation and reporting require banks to have:

  • strong governance and architecture
  • accurate, complete, timely and adaptable risk data aggregation
  • reports that are accurate, comprehensive, clear and timely, including under stress.

In practice that means a lineage-backed, quality-controlled path from source to report, with owners and reconciliation. Supervisors (the ECB, the RBI and others) continue to flag gaps in BCBS 239 compliance, so treat it as a design requirement.

Governance for AI#

  • Know the provenance and rights of training and RAG data (consent, licences, legal basis).
  • Track datasets → features → models → decisions lineage. Keep model cards and datasheets.
  • Apply the same classification and access policies to embeddings and vector stores as to their source documents.
  • The EU AI Act requires data governance for high-risk AI (training data quality, bias examination). See FinTech regulations. ISO/IEC 42001 provides an AI management system standard.

Common mistakes#

Common mistakeGovernance as a committee, not a capability

Policies that are not automated in the platform are ignored. Encode classification, quality and access as code.

Common mistakeA catalog nobody updates

Manual documentation goes stale in weeks. Harvest metadata and lineage automatically.

Common mistakeQuality checks only at the end

Detecting problems in the gold report means the damage is done. Check at ingestion and between layers.

Quick check#

Data owner vs data steward? The owner is the business person accountable for a domain (access, quality targets). The steward manages definitions, quality rules and issues day to day.

What is OpenLineage? An open standard for emitting lineage events from pipelines and engines, collected by catalogs to show how data flows and transforms.

Name four data quality dimensions. Completeness, validity, accuracy, consistency, uniqueness and timeliness.

What does BCBS 239 require? Strong governance and infrastructure for risk data, with accurate, complete, timely and adaptable aggregation and reporting, in practice backed by lineage and quality controls.

Why govern embeddings like the source documents? Embeddings and retrieved chunks can reveal the source content, so the same access and classification rules must apply.

Summary#

  • Governance = roles + policies + automated tooling, so data is known, trusted, protected and used properly.
  • Catalog and lineage (OpenLineage) are the backbone; quality checks run throughout the pipeline.
  • Contracts shift quality upstream; master and reference data need golden records.
  • Regulation (BCBS 239, the EU AI Act) makes governance a design requirement.
