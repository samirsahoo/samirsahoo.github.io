Model Selection for the Enterprise

How to choose LLMs: capability tiers, open-weight vs proprietary, benchmarks and their limits, a weighted scoring matrix, model routing and cascades, and avoiding lock-in.

Simple definition#

Model selection is choosing which LLM (or which mix of LLMs) serves each use case, based on quality for the task, cost, latency, data-protection requirements and operational fit. In an enterprise it is not a one-time choice: models change every few months, so the architecture must make swapping cheap.

Why it matters#

The difference between models on your task can be large, and price per token varies by more than 50× between the smallest and largest models. Picking the biggest model for everything wastes money; picking the cheapest for everything fails on hard tasks.

The selection criteria#

Criterion Questions to ask
Task quality How does it score on our evaluation set? Reasoning, extraction accuracy, code, tool use, multilingual?
Cost Price per million input/output tokens; caching and batch discounts; total cost at projected volume.
Latency TTFT and tokens/s at our prompt sizes; is streaming supported?
Context window Fits our largest realistic prompt with headroom?
Capabilities Tool calling, structured outputs, vision, audio, reasoning mode, embeddings.
Data protection Data retention, training-on-your-data terms, regional hosting, certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001), private networking.
Deployment Provider API, your cloud (Bedrock, Azure, Vertex), or self-hosted?
Licence For open-weight models: commercial use allowed? usage restrictions?
Safety Refusal behaviour, jailbreak resistance, content filters.
Vendor and roadmap Deprecation policy, SLAs, support, financial stability.

Model tiers#

Proprietary vs open-weight#

Proprietary (API) Open-weight (self-host or managed)
Examples Claude, GPT, Gemini families Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek families
Peak quality Usually highest Close behind, especially at mid-tier
Operations None — pay per token You run GPUs, scaling, patching (unless a cloud hosts it)
Data control Contractual (retention, region) Full — data never leaves your environment
Customisation Prompting; some offer fine-tuning Full fine-tuning, LoRA, quantization
Cost profile Variable, scales with usage Fixed GPU cost; cheaper at high steady volume
Lock-in API differences between vendors Low for the model, but you own the platform

Note: “open-weight” means the weights are downloadable; licences differ and are not always open source in the OSI sense.

Benchmarks: useful, but not sufficient#

Public benchmarks (MMLU-style knowledge tests, GPQA for hard science, SWE-bench for coding, tool-use and agent benchmarks, LMArena-style human preference rankings) are good for shortlisting. They are poor for deciding, because:

  • They may not resemble your task, documents or language.
  • Models can be over-optimised for popular benchmarks (“contamination”).
  • They ignore your latency, cost and data constraints.

The decision must come from your own evaluation set: 50–500 representative inputs with expected outputs or grading criteria, scored automatically and spot-checked by experts. See RAG evaluation for the methods.

A repeatable selection process#

Weighted scoring example — customer-support RAG assistant

Criterion (weight) Model A frontier Model B mid-tier Model C open-weight 70B
Answer quality on eval set (40%) 9 8 7
Cost at volume (20%) 4 8 7
Latency (15%) 6 8 7
Data protection fit (15%) 7 7 10
Operational effort (10%) 9 9 4
Weighted total 7.25 7.95 7.15

Model B wins for this use case. A legal-analysis use case with different weights might pick Model A.

Model routing and cascades#

You rarely need one model for everything. An AI gateway or router sends each request to the cheapest model that can handle it.

  • Routing — classify the request first, then choose a model.
  • Cascade — try the cheap model first; escalate if validation or confidence fails.
  • Fallback — on provider outage or rate limit, switch to an equivalent model from another provider.

Routing commonly cuts cost by 40–70% when most traffic is simple.

Avoiding lock-in#

  • Call models through an abstraction: Spring AI’s ChatModel, or an AI gateway with an OpenAI-compatible API.
  • Keep prompts, evaluation sets and tool definitions model-agnostic and in version control.
  • Store embeddings with the embedding model name and version; changing the embedding model means re-indexing.
  • Re-run the evaluation suite before switching models — prompts often need adjustment.

Common mistakes#

Common mistakeChoosing by leaderboard or hype

A model at the top of a public leaderboard can underperform on your documents and language. Decide with your own evaluation set.

Common mistakeOne model for every use case

The biggest model for a sentiment label wastes money; the smallest for legal reasoning fails. Match the tier to the task and route.

Common mistakeNo fallback model

Provider outages and rate limits happen. A second, pre-evaluated model on a different provider keeps critical features running.

Review questions#

How would you choose an LLM for a new enterprise use case? Define the task and success metrics; apply hard filters (data residency, licence, required capabilities, context size); shortlist a few models using benchmarks and price; evaluate them on our own dataset for quality, latency and cost; score with weights agreed with the business; choose a primary and fallback; and re-evaluate when new models appear.

Open-weight or proprietary? Proprietary APIs give the best quality and zero operations; open-weight gives full data control, customisation and lower cost at high steady volume. Many enterprises use proprietary models through their cloud provider for most work and open-weight models for sensitive or very high-volume tasks.

What is model routing and why use it? Sending each request to the cheapest model capable of handling it, using rules or a classifier, sometimes with escalation on low confidence. It cuts cost and latency without hurting quality on hard requests.

Why are public benchmarks not enough? They do not reflect your data, task or constraints, can be contaminated, and ignore cost and latency. Use them to shortlist and your own evaluation set to decide.

How do you avoid vendor lock-in? Abstraction layer or gateway, model-agnostic prompts and tool schemas, an evaluation suite to qualify replacements quickly, and a tested fallback provider.

Summary#

  • Select per use case, on task quality, cost, latency, data protection and operational fit.
  • Shortlist with benchmarks; decide with your own evaluation set and weighted scoring.
  • Use tiers: frontier for hard reasoning, mid-tier for most work, small models for high-volume simple tasks.
  • Route and cascade to save cost; keep a fallback model; abstract the provider.
