Agentic AI Strategy and Operating Model

How an enterprise moves from chatbots to agents: a maturity model, use-case selection and portfolio, build vs buy, value and cost measurement, the AgentOps operating model, workforce and change considerations, and a 12-month roadmap.

Simple definition#

An agentic AI strategy decides where agents should do work in the organisation, how much autonomy they get, what platform they run on, how value and risk are measured, and how people and processes change around them. It turns isolated pilots into a governed, scalable capability.

Maturity model#

Level Typical capabilities Platform needs
1 Assist Knowledge assistants, drafting, coding assistants AI gateway, RAG, acceptable-use policy
2 Automate steps Document processing, classification, summaries inside workflows Evaluation in CI, LLMOps, structured output
3 Act with oversight Agents with write tools, HITL approvals MCP servers, agent identity, guardrails, audit
4 Orchestrate Cross-domain agents, A2A, shared agent registry MCP/A2A gateways, durable runtime, agent registry
5 Autonomous ops Agents own processes with SLAs Mature evaluation, incident response, governance at scale

Most large enterprises in 2026 operate at levels 2–3, with selected level-4 processes.

Choosing use cases#

For a detailed impact and feasibility scorecard, the prioritisation matrix and the A.G.E.N.T. design framework, see the agent building playbook.

Score each candidate on:

Dimension Questions
Value Volume × time saved, revenue, risk reduction, customer experience
Agent fit Is the work varied, judgment-based, multi-system? (If fixed, use a workflow or RPA)
Data & tool readiness APIs or MCP servers available? Clean data and permissions?
Risk Regulatory tier, harm if wrong, reversibility of actions
Measurability Clear success metric and evaluation data?

Good early agentic candidates: IT and HR service desks, software engineering, customer-service case handling with approvals, document-heavy back-office processes, research and analysis for internal teams.

Build, buy or configure?#

Option When
Buy agents inside SaaS (CRM, ITSM, ERP vendors) The process lives in that SaaS and differentiation is low
Configure low-code agents Departmental, moderate-risk automations
Build on a framework + platform Differentiating processes, cross-system work, strict control and governance

Whatever you choose, insist on open protocols (MCP, A2A), exportable traces, evaluation access and identity integration, so bought and built agents can work together and be governed centrally.

Measuring value and cost#

Metric Example
Outcome Cases resolved without human touch; cycle time; error rate
Quality Task success rate on evaluation set; customer satisfaction; human override rate
Efficiency Cost per completed task (tokens + platform + human review) vs baseline
Risk Policy violations, incidents, escalation rate
Adoption Active users, tasks delegated, trust surveys

Key conceptMeasure cost per successful outcome

Token cost per call is misleading for agents. Compare total cost per successfully completed task, including retries, human review and failures, with the current process.

Operating model: AgentOps#

  • Agent product owners in domain teams own outcomes, autonomy levels and evaluation sets.
  • Platform team provides runtime, MCP/A2A gateways, agent registry, identity, observability and guardrails as paved roads.
  • AgentOps runs agents like services: SLOs, dashboards, on-call, incident playbooks, cost management.
  • Governance sets risk tiers, approves autonomy increases, audits.

People and change#

  • Redesign work around human + agent roles: who supervises, approves, handles exceptions.
  • Train staff to delegate to, review and correct agents (AI literacy is also a legal duty under the EU AI Act).
  • Communicate openly about impact on jobs; involve works councils where required.
  • Track trust: override rates and user feedback show whether autonomy can grow.

A 12-month roadmap#

Quarter Focus
Q1 Platform foundations: AI gateway, agent framework standard, MCP servers for 3–5 key systems, evaluation tooling, governance tiers; 2 pilot agents at level 2–3
Q2 Production for pilots with HITL; agent identity and registry; AgentOps dashboards; skills repository
Q3 Scale to 5–10 agents; A2A between domains; progressive autonomy on proven actions; cost-per-outcome reporting
Q4 Cross-domain orchestrated processes; continuous evaluation and red teaming; review of operating model and value

Common mistakes#

Common mistakePilot purgatory

Dozens of demos, none in production, because platform, evaluation and governance foundations were never built. Build the paved road with the first two use cases.

Common mistakeAutonomy before evidence

Granting agents full autonomy on day one. Increase autonomy per action as evaluation and production metrics prove reliability.

Review questions#

How would you prioritise agentic use cases? By value, agent fit (varied, judgment-based, multi-system work), data and tool readiness, risk and measurability — starting with high-value, feasible, lower-risk cases and building the platform through them.

How do you measure an agent’s ROI? Total cost per successfully completed task — tokens, platform, human review, failures — against the current process cost, plus quality, risk and adoption metrics.

What does an agent operating model look like? A governance council setting policy and risk tiers, a platform team providing runtime, gateways, registry and evaluation tooling, domain teams owning agents and outcomes, and AgentOps running agents with SLOs, monitoring and incident response.

How do you decide when to increase an agent’s autonomy? Per action, based on evidence: evaluation results, production success and override rates, incident history, and the action’s impact and reversibility — approved through governance.

Summary#

  • Maturity runs from assist → automate steps → act with oversight → orchestrate → autonomous operations.
  • Choose use cases by value, agent fit, readiness, risk and measurability.
  • Buy, configure or build — but require open protocols, traces, evaluation and identity integration.
  • Measure cost per successful outcome; run agents with AgentOps; grow autonomy on evidence.
